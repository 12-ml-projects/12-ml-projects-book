I have long wanted to write a book about machine learning. I write this book because the subject has intrigued me from the moment I first heard about it at university from a friend who was writing his own generative adversial network (GAN) for sport. Little did I know then, in 2018, that machine learning was going to be the "next big thing".

Now that machine learning is everywhere, it has stirred up a complex dialogue in the public sphere, now being something of a famous or infamous thing due to how it has been used, misused, or wittingly abused. I don't know enough to add to the debate. I will only say that I believe that, in spite of even our best intentions, in this field we "engineers" really do know "enough to be dangerous", and we must continue to educate ourselves on and on to handle risks safely and bear them responsibly. But here we are; machine learning is here to stay and it is—in and of itself—quite an interesting thing to read and write on.

I wrote this book because I wanted to learn, and I learned a lot. The book brings together what I know from my experience with ML, dozens of conversations with friends in research, and shy of a hundred or so books I've had the pleasure to read. Since this book is written in the author's image, I trust that you will read in it some of the joy I've had writing it. That's all that matters, anyway.

## About This Book

This book is web-native. Web technologies allow me to bake search, hyperlinks and custom-built web components—code viewers, simulations, games, svg images—natively into the book. The book is fully open-sourced and welcomes public review and contributions on GitHub, with attribution on the attributors page.
